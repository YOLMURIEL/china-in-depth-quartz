一带一路 (Yídài Yílù): The Belt and Road Initiative

中国的“一带一路”倡议

The Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) is one of the most significant concepts in contemporary Chinese international relations and diplomatic discourse. It refers to China’s proposal to develop economic and infrastructure connectivity through land and maritime routes, drawing inspiration from the historical Silk Road.

This article examines the initiative from three perspectives: its historical and geopolitical context, its Chinese terminology and its translation into English and Spanish.

1. What is the Belt and Road Initiative?

The Belt and Road Initiative was introduced by Chinese President Xi Jinping in 2013 through two proposals:

  • The Silk Road Economic Belt (丝绸之路经济带).
  • The 21st-Century Maritime Silk Road (21世纪海上丝绸之路).

The first proposal was presented during a visit to Kazakhstan in September 2013, while the second was introduced in Indonesia in October of the same year.

The initiative seeks to strengthen international connectivity through infrastructure development, trade, investment, financial cooperation and cultural exchanges.

It is not limited to transport infrastructure. It also encompasses economic cooperation, international relations and people-to-people exchanges.

2. The meaning of 一带一路

The Chinese expression 一带一路 (Yídài Yílù) is composed of four characters:

ChinesePinyinMeaning
一yīOne
带dàiBelt
一yīOne
路lùRoad / Route

Its literal meaning is One Belt, One Road.

However, the established English name is The Belt and Road Initiative (BRI).

In Spanish, the most common translation is:

La Iniciativa de la Franja y la Ruta.

The term is a shortened expression referring to the two major components of the initiative: the economic belt and the maritime silk road.

3. The two main components

3.1. The Silk Road Economic Belt

丝绸之路经济带

This land-based component seeks to improve connectivity between China, Central Asia, West Asia and Europe.

It involves the development of:

  • Railways and international transport corridors.
  • Roads and logistics infrastructure.
  • Energy and pipeline networks.
  • Industrial and economic cooperation zones.
  • Trade connections between Asian and European markets.

3.2. The 21st-Century Maritime Silk Road

21世纪海上丝绸之路

The maritime component focuses on sea routes connecting China with Southeast Asia, South Asia, Africa and Europe.

Its main areas of cooperation include:

  • Port infrastructure.
  • Maritime transport.
  • Logistics and international trade.
  • Port connectivity.
  • Maritime economic cooperation.

Together, these two components form the geographical and economic framework of the Belt and Road Initiative.

4. Five areas of cooperation

Chinese official documents frequently refer to five key areas of connectivity:

These expressions are particularly relevant for translators because they form part of the recurring vocabulary of Chinese diplomatic and institutional discourse.

民心相通 (mínxīn xiāngtōng) is especially interesting from a translation perspective. It refers to mutual understanding and connections between peoples, rather than simply economic or institutional cooperation.

5. Geopolitical and economic dimensions

The Belt and Road Initiative has implications for international trade, investment, transport infrastructure and China’s relations with other countries.

Its geographical scope connects different regions of Asia, Europe and Africa through economic corridors and maritime routes.

From an international relations perspective, the initiative can be studied through several dimensions:

  • China’s international economic relations.
  • Infrastructure financing and investment.
  • Regional integration and connectivity.
  • China–Europe relations.
  • International trade and logistics.
  • Diplomatic cooperation.

Its implementation has also generated debate about financing conditions, debt sustainability, transparency, environmental impacts and the distribution of economic benefits.

The World Bank has highlighted potential gains in trade and connectivity, while stressing that these benefits depend on appropriate policy reforms and the management of economic, environmental and social risks.

Therefore, the initiative should be examined through different institutional, economic and geopolitical perspectives.

6. Translation and terminology: Chinese–English–Spanish

The translation of 一带一路 provides an interesting example of how political terminology is established and standardised internationally.

Official terminology

LanguageEstablished expression
Chinese一带一路倡议
PinyinYídài Yílù Chàngyì
EnglishThe Belt and Road Initiative
English abbreviationBRI
SpanishLa Iniciativa de la Franja y la Ruta

The Chinese authorities issued guidance in 2015 standardising the English terminology.

One important distinction concerns the word 倡议 (chàngyì):

  • 倡议: initiative.
  • 战略: strategy.
  • 项目: project.
  • 计划: programme / plan.

The official English terminology uses initiative, rather than strategy, project or programme.

This distinction matters in diplomatic translation because these expressions are not interchangeable. Each one conveys a different institutional and political meaning.

Translation challenges

The expression 一带一路 raises several linguistic questions:

  1. Should the translator preserve the literal meaning or use the established institutional name?
  2. How should Chinese political terminology be translated consistently across languages?
  3. What cultural and historical references are embedded in the expression?
  4. How can the translator distinguish official terminology from media terminology?

For professional translation, the established name should generally be preferred over an improvised literal translation.

8. Why is it relevant to China studies?

The Belt and Road Initiative provides a framework for understanding contemporary China’s international economic and diplomatic engagement.

It also offers a valuable case study for students and researchers working in:

  • Chinese–Spanish translation.
  • Political and diplomatic terminology.
  • International relations.
  • International economics.
  • Geopolitics.
  • Infrastructure and urban development.

Its interdisciplinary nature makes it a particularly useful topic for connecting language studies with political, economic and technological research.
